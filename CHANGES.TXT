truth-table.nvim changes
========================

- rewrite.lua: `sites(ast)` lists where a collapsing law can apply and
  makes its own list, as trees.operands and predicate.variables do; the
  recursion is an inner `visit`.
- predicate.lua: a token carries the bytes it was read from, as `span`, so
  `tokenize` answers the tokens or nil and the reason, and `parse_predicate`
  places every error by a byte, taking the byte after the source as an
  option for a parser that runs out of tokens (the byte after the last
  token without it) and `node_spans` for the located parse. result.lua:
  `map` is `bind` under the name that says `fn` always answers. The README's
  tokenizer wrapper sets `predicate.tokenize` alone, which the parser reads
  through the module table.
- preview.lua is the pending preview alone: the rewrite kinds, the
  per-buffer state, its extmarks, invalidation on edit, and applying in
  place or as a step. The expression under the cursor is source.lua's
  (`at(buf)`: a side of a derivation line, or a heading, with `replace`
  and `step` to write a rewrite back); what a write does is write.lua's
  (`prepare` plans it, `in_place` and `step` carry it out, `lit` makes the
  marks of lit ranges); the <Space> and <CR> keys that hold while a preview
  is pending are transient.lua's (`engage(buf, keys)`, `release`,
  `forget`); and the menu of every rewrite is menu.lua's `choose`.
  `preview.dismiss(buf)` is public, for the menu.
- init.lua: the user commands are one table, COMMANDS, each entry its
  name, what it runs and its options (nargs, range, bang, desc), beside
  KEYMAPS; a keymap names the command it runs, and setup() checks the name
  is one COMMANDS defines, so a key cannot point at a command that is not
  there. A keymap that does more than run one command (the new-table
  mapping, the expand prefill, the backward swap) spells its own right-hand
  side. preview.lua is required with the other modules.
- marks.lua: a highlight mark is one record, { row, col, end_col, group,
  priority }, painted and read back through `paint` and `read`; edit.lua's
  undo-tracked marks, preview.lua's lit terms and the tutor's colour spans
  are lists of it. preview.lua lights an expression's ranges through
  `lit(at, ranges, group)`, with the priority per group and the pending
  preview's above both.
- text.lua: `trim`, shared by core, predicate and markdown. markdown.lua
  finds a line's pipes once, in `pipes`; derivation.lua trims the sides
  once, in `trimmed`; karnaugh.lua parses each input heading once per
  cover; the tutor passes its place record, { lesson, step }, through to
  tutor_page.render and tutor_page.progress, which takes `furthest` as its
  own argument.
- trees.lua: `renderer(selected, regions, positions)` is the renderer over
  one traversal's context, and `render(node, offset)` what it returns;
  tutor_vellum.lua passes vellum's renderer and theme as one toolkit and
  its rendering as one page, { markdown, lines, blocks }, which
  tutor_spans.place and `anchored` read.
- table_model.lua: `ENCODINGS` spells the cells of each encoding, the one
  place they are spelled: `parse` reads them, render_rows and the Karnaugh
  map write them. `parse(headers, rows)` is the one validator, run by the
  Markdown codec on the cells it reads, and `headings` is its check on the
  headings alone, which replace_heading runs on a renamed one. The edits
  after it (append_columns, drop_row, drop_column, toggle) take a valid
  table and return one, leaving the input as it was; a table core builds
  carries its encoding from the start. append_columns, toggle and
  markdown.format always answer.
- trees.lua: the glue between two operands, and the set of every glue text
  that joins two lit regions, come from the operator table.
- rewrite.lua: a rewrite's `change` record carries `text`, the text of
  `after`, which preview.lua and `moves` read. Inside the module every
  rewrite drafts what it finds as `{ tree, law, consumed, produced }`, each
  draft in order of preference with the reason when there are none; the
  command takes the first draft and `moves` takes them all. A collapsing
  law answers with the operand lists it finds. `substitute` is
  `trees.transform` with one replacement, and `trees.children` is public,
  read by the canonical form's traversals and by rewrite.lua's path walks.

- Previews light both ends of a rewrite. While one is pending, the terms the
  law takes are lit on the line in TruthTableConsumed (a default link to
  DiagnosticWarn): the operands a commute moves, each occurrence a factoring
  pulls out, the operand distributing expands and the group it enters, the

- A derivation's justification bars stay in one column: a written step
  realigns the steps above it, in the same undo entry as the step, and
  :TruthTableAlign does the same for a derivation typed by hand, from any of

    result can be rewritten again without reparsing. truth-table.edit writes
    text and marks as one patch and keeps the marks per undo sequence
    number. rewrite.moves entries carry each move's transaction.
    derivation.block(lines, row) finds the derivation around a row and
    derivation.aligned(block, display_width) measures it.

- The reference lesson (now 19) lists the five stages and the rule for the

- Expressions are written back in one canonical form, in headings, previews,
  derivation steps and Karnaugh formula lines: an operand that is itself a
  binary expression is parenthesised, a run of ∧ or of ∨ is written flat, and
  no other parenthesis is written. So `a and b or c` renders as (a ∧ b) ∨ c
  where it rendered as a ∧ b ∨ c; (a ∧ b) ∧ c and a ∧ (b ∧ c) both render as
  a ∧ b ∧ c; a nested →, ⊕ or ⇔ is always marked ((a ⇔ b) ⇔ c, where it
  rendered as a ⇔ b ⇔ c); and parentheses in the source no longer survive
  rendering: ((a)) ∧ (¬(b)) is a ∧ ¬b.
- Add predicate.canonical(ast): the tree transform behind the rendering. It
  returns one tree for every grouping of an expression, with a run of ∧ or ∨
  nested to the left and paren nodes exactly where the text has parentheses.
  Rewrites return canonical trees, and rewrite.lua drops the code that
  regrouped its results and chose their parentheses.

  their own copies.

- Add :TruthTableSimplify (<leader>ttz): preview one collapsing law at the
  nearest match to the cursor and name it. The laws are complement (A ∨ ¬A to

  each with its dual, plus a negated constant and a double negation. The
  search runs from a law involving the operand under the cursor, to one inside
  what the cursor selects, to one in a chain around it, to one anywhere.
- Every rewrite names its law. A preview ends in `| by <law>`, and
  :TruthTableApplyStep writes that after the step, with the bars of a
  derivation in one column:
      ≡ b ∧ (a ∨ ¬a)                | by distributivity

  rewrites leave alone. :TruthTableApply on a justified line adds the new law
  to its justification.

- The words `true` and `false` are read as ⊤ and ⊥, the way `and` is read as
  ∧, and are reserved: `:TruthTable true false` is now an expression (and a
  parse error) where it made two variables, and an existing column with either
  name is reached from an expression by its :hN reference.
